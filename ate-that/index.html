How it works

Hold, dictate, hold again.

Built for the flow you actually want: log food the moment you think about it, even while using another app. Control Center works as a backup, and the app can log meals too.

1

Use the Action Button

Hold the iPhone Action Button to start recording, say what you ate, then hold again to submit. Ate That shows progress in Live Activities and the Dynamic Island.

2

Get useful estimates

The app estimates calories, protein, fat, carbs, and alcohol. It treats calorie tracking as an approximation — because portions, labels, and recipes are approximate too.

3

Review and correct

Open meal details to inspect the transcript and item breakdown. Edit meals by voice, adjust the meal time, or delete entries that don’t belong.

Built for iPhone

Designed around the quickest possible capture.

Logging starts from the places iPhone users already reach for: Action Button, Control Center, Dynamic Island, Lock Screen, and the app home screen.

Trust the record

See why the estimate happened.

Each meal shows the item breakdown, total calories, nutrients, transcript, Apple Health export status, and prompt version metadata. The point isn’t perfect math — it’s a clear, reviewable record.

  • Calories are calculated from item estimates.
  • Alcohol is stored in grams and shown as drinks.
  • Empty or unclear recordings are not saved as meals.

Privacy

Local meal history, protected processing.

Your meal history stays on your iPhone. Sign in with Apple secures access to Ate That's service. When you submit a meal, the service sends the recording and relevant meal context to OpenAI for transcription and estimation. Ate That does not retain submitted recordings.

Account-linked transcripts, structured results, request details, and private food preferences are retained until you delete your account. Apple Health export remains optional and is not sent to Ate That's servers or OpenAI.
